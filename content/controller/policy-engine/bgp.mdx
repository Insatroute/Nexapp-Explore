BGP peering. Beyond the router ID and AS it carries the timers and the BFD settings that decide how fast a dead peer is noticed.

Scope is the same across the Policy Engine: a policy with **Apply fleet-wide** set reaches every device, otherwise it is narrowed to an organization. There is deliberately no device picker — configuring a single router is what that router's own CPE page is for.

<Callout type="info">Served by the shared `PolicyEngine` component via the `/policy-engine/:tab` route. Facts below describe that component, which renders several tabs.</Callout>

## What you can do here

### Actions

- **Delete resource** — Removes a policy via `DELETE /api/v1/nsbond/bgp-config/<id>/`.
- **Edit resource** — Creates and edits a policy through the shared resource form. Fields: Router ID, Router AS, Redistribute connected/static, Keepalive interval, Hold time, Log neighbor changes, Graceful restart, Deterministic MED, Default local preference, and BFD (min RX/TX interval, detect multiplier).

### Reading and lookups

- **Browse and search resource** — Lists policies from `GET /api/v1/nsbond/bgp-config/`, with columns Name, Router ID, AS, Scope, State.

**Opens:** `ResourceTable` — some actions above are performed in it.
